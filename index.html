📘 How Tokenization Works

Tokenization is the process of breaking text into smaller units called tokens. It is a foundational step in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and large language models (LLMs). Different tokenization strategies produce different vocabularies and encoded representations.

🔤 Word-Level Tokenization

Splits text on whitespace and punctuation boundaries. Each word and each punctuation mark becomes its own token. This is the simplest approach but leads to very large vocabularies and cannot handle out-of-vocabulary (OOV) words.

  • Input: "I love AI!"
  • Tokens: ["I", "love", "AI", "!"]

🔡 Character-Level Tokenization

Splits text into individual characters, including spaces and punctuation. The vocabulary is tiny (just the unique characters), but sequences become very long, losing word-level meaning.

  • Input: "AI"
  • Tokens: ["A", "I"]

🧩 Subword Tokenization (BPE)

Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) is used by models like GPT-2, GPT-3, and RoBERTa. It strikes a balance between word and character levels:

  1. Start with a character-level vocabulary.
  2. Count every adjacent pair of symbols in the corpus.
  3. Merge the most frequent pair into a new symbol.
  4. Repeat for a fixed number of merge operations.

This tool trains BPE on your input text, so you can observe how the algorithm discovers common subword units. Pre-trained LLM tokenizers (e.g., GPT-4's cl100k_base) are trained on billions of tokens and have 50K–100K merge rules — the BPE here is an educational, faithful implementation of the same algorithm at smaller scale.

🆚 Educational vs. Pre-trained Tokenizers

This tool implements a genuine educational tokenizer. The word and character methods are deterministic. The BPE method trains on the provided input, faithfully following the original BPE algorithm. Pre-trained tokenizers (like those in Hugging Face's tokenizers library) are trained on massive corpora and have fixed vocabularies — they will produce different subword splits because their merge rules were learned from billions of sentences.
